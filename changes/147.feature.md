Covenant tests on the definitions of the loan agreement. For each covenant you give the numerator and the denominator as the agreement defines them, the threshold, whether it is a maximum or a minimum and whether the limit itself is allowed, and the test date. The rules that change from one agreement to another are explicit choices, with no default: what happens when the denominator is zero or negative (breach, pass or not tested), and, when the agreement says so, the rounding of the ratio before the comparison, for example to two decimals. Fairmark returns for each covenant whether it is passed, breached or not tested with the reason, the tested value and the headroom, in the unit of the ratio and as a share of the threshold, positive when the covenant is respected and negative when it is breached. The outcome is decided exactly, so a ratio that differs from the threshold only in the last digits is never misclassified.
