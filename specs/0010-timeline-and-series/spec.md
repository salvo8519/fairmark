## Problem
Analysts build a model by stating a start date, a frequency and a number of periods; the
spreadsheet then produces the period dates and a row of values grown from a base. An AI agent
asked to do the same today must compute the dates itself (month ends, February, leap years)
and the projected values itself, before passing them to `tvm.xnpv` or `tvm.xirr`. These are
exactly the computations where an agent or a spreadsheet goes wrong silently: 31 January plus
one month, a schedule that drifts from 31 to 28 after February, an annual growth applied
unchanged to monthly periods, a first value that is or is not already grown. Two small
deterministic utilities remove that work from the agent: `dates.schedule` builds the dates and
`series.project` builds the values, with every convention explicit and outputs whose shapes
feed `tvm.xnpv`, `tvm.xirr` and, later, the DCF with dated periods (#167).

## Scope
**In scope:**
- `dates.schedule`: from a start date, a frequency and a number of periods, the start and end
  date of every period (each end is the next start), with an explicit end-of-month rule; the
  year fraction of every period and from the start date under a stated day count; the payment
  date of every period under a stated timing.
- `series.project`: from a base value, a growth rule (constant, linear between two rates, one
  rate per period) and a timeline (a number of periods with an optional frequency, or the
  timeline returned by `dates.schedule`), the projected value of every period; conversion of an
  annual growth rate to a sub-annual period with an explicit convention.
- Frequencies of 12, 6, 4, 3, 2 and 1 periods a year, defined once in the core and shared by
  the compounding list (`rates.convert`, `tvm.xnpv`, `tvm.xirr`, `loans.*`) and the loan payment
  frequency (`loans.*`).

**Out of scope (future extensions, input designed to accept them as additive options):**
- Business-day calendars and business-day conventions (following, modified following,
  preceding): every date of version 1 is unadjusted.
- A termination (end) date instead of a number of periods; short or long stub periods, at the
  start or at the end; backward generation from the end date.
- Frequencies that are not whole months (weekly, daily) and frequencies of more than one year.
- Mid-period payment dates (a mid-period date needs a day-rounding rule).
- Rounding of projected values; growth rules other than the three above (for example
  compound annual growth to a target value, or growth driven by another series).
- Any change to `dcf.*`: the DCF with dated periods is specified by #167.

## User stories
- As an analyst, I want the quarter ends of the next five years from 31 December 2025, so that
  my dated flows fall on 31 March, 30 June, 30 September and 31 December without typing them.
- As an analyst, I want a monthly schedule that starts on 31 January to keep month ends (28 or
  29 February, then 31 March) and never drift to the 28th, so that the dates match my ledger.
- As an analyst, I want revenues of 1,000 grown at 5% a year, projected monthly, with the
  annual-to-monthly conversion stated, so that I know whether twelve months compound to 5%.
- As an AI agent, I want to pass the timeline returned by `dates.schedule` to `series.project`
  and the dated values it returns to `tvm.xnpv` unchanged, so that I never retype a date or a
  number.
- As an AI agent, I want the engine to refuse an annual growth on a monthly timeline when I did
  not state how to convert it, so that I ask the user instead of guessing.

## Requirements (EARS)

### dates.schedule
- **R1** — WHEN given a start date, a frequency and a number of periods n, THE ENGINE SHALL
  return n contiguous periods: the start date of period 1 is the start date; the end date of
  period k is the date d_k generated by R2 and R3 for k = 1..n; the start date of period k + 1
  is the end date of period k.
- **R2** — THE ENGINE SHALL generate every date from the start date, not from the previous
  date: d_k = start date + k × (months per period) months, with the day of month set by the
  end-of-month rule; so that a day of month shortened in one period (31 January to 29 February)
  is restored in the next period (31 March) and the schedule never drifts.
- **R3** — THE ENGINE SHALL accept the frequencies `monthly` (12 periods a year, 1 month per
  period), `bimonthly` (6, 2 months), `quarterly` (4, 3 months), `four_monthly` (3, 4 months),
  `semi_annual` (2, 6 months) and `annual` (1, 12 months), and SHALL report the periods per year
  and the months per period.
- **R4** — THE ENGINE SHALL apply one of two end-of-month rules, and report which one applied
  and whether it changed any date:
  - `same_day`: the day of month of every date equals the day of the start date, or the last
    day of the month when that month is shorter (31 January monthly: 29 February 2024, 31 March,
    30 April);
  - `month_end`: WHEN the start date is the last day of its month, every date is the last day of
    its month (30 June quarterly: 30 September, 31 December, 31 March); otherwise as
    `same_day`.
  The two rules differ only when the start date is the last day of a month shorter than 31
  days. The default is set by Open question 1 (proposal: `month_end`, documented and reported).
- **R5** — WHEN a day count (the five day counts of SPEC-0002 R2) is given, THE ENGINE SHALL
  return the year fraction of every period, from its start date to its end date, and the year
  fraction from the start date of the schedule to the end date of every period, each computed
  by the day-count function of `dates.year_fraction` (no second implementation). WHEN no day
  count is given, THE ENGINE SHALL return no year fraction and SHALL state in
  `explain.conventions` that none was computed (Open question 2).
- **R6** — WHEN a payment timing is given, `end_of_period` or `beginning_of_period`, THE ENGINE
  SHALL return the payment date of every period: its end date or its start date respectively.
  WHEN no timing is given, it SHALL return no payment date. `mid_period` is not a value of
  version 1.
- **R7** — THE ENGINE SHALL return the dates as a `timeline` object holding the frequency, the
  start dates and the end dates, so that the object can be passed unchanged to `series.project`
  (R17).
- **R8** — THE ENGINE SHALL produce unadjusted dates (no business-day adjustment) and SHALL
  report it in `explain.conventions`; fields for an end date, a calendar, a business-day
  convention or a stub are not part of version 1 and SHALL be refused as unknown fields
  (`FM-E001`).
- **R9** — IF the number of periods is 0 or above 10000, THEN THE ENGINE SHALL return `FM-E001`
  naming `/number_of_periods`; IF the start date is malformed, impossible or out of range, THEN
  it SHALL return `FM-E009` naming `/start_date`; IF a generated date falls after 2199-12-31,
  THEN it SHALL return `FM-E009` naming `/number_of_periods`, the last valid period and the
  first date out of range.
- **R10** — THE ENGINE SHALL include in `explain`: the generation rule stated once with its
  formula; the frequency with its periods per year and months per period; the end-of-month rule
  and its effect; the day count or the statement that none was given; the timing or the
  statement that none was given; and, when a day count is given, the year fraction of the first
  period with the numbers substituted (the day-count formula of `dates.year_fraction`) and the
  sum of the year fractions. The per-period numbers are carried by the output vectors, as for
  the rows of `loans.amortisation_schedule` (SPEC-0009 Decision 8); the number of steps is
  bounded and independent of n.

### series.project
- **R11** — WHEN given a base value V_0, a growth rule, a base period and a timeline of n
  periods, THE ENGINE SHALL return the projected value of every period 1..n by the recurrence
  V_k = V_{k−1} × (1 + g_k), computed period by period (never by a closed-form power), where
  g_k is the growth rate per period into period k after any conversion (R15).
- **R12** — THE ENGINE SHALL require the base period, with no default:
  - `period_0`: the base value is the value before the first period (for example the last
    actual year); V_1 = V_0 × (1 + g_1); n growth rates are applied;
  - `period_1`: the base value is the value of period 1; V_1 = V_0; growth rates are applied
    into periods 2..n only (n − 1 rates).
  The base value itself is never an element of the returned values, which are always the values
  of periods 1..n.
- **R13** — WHEN the growth rule is `constant` with rate g, THE ENGINE SHALL apply g to every
  growth step.
- **R14** — WHEN the growth rule is `linear` from `from_growth` to `to_growth` over s growth
  steps (s = n for `period_0`, n − 1 for `period_1`), THE ENGINE SHALL apply
  g_j = from_growth + (to_growth − from_growth) × j / s for step j = 1..s, so that `to_growth` is
  reached in the last step and `from_growth` is the growth before the first step: the rule of
  SPEC-0001 Decision 4 (Open question 5). The rule applies to rising and falling growth.
- **R15** — WHEN the growth rule is `per_period` with a list of rates, THE ENGINE SHALL apply
  them in order, one per growth step; IF the list does not have exactly s elements, THEN it
  SHALL return `FM-E001` naming `/growth/per_period/growth_rates` with the expected and the
  actual count and the base period that sets the count.
- **R16** — THE ENGINE SHALL interpret every growth rate of the rule according to the growth
  basis:
  - `per_period`: the rate is the growth per period of the timeline, used as given;
  - `annual_compound`: the rate g is an annual growth; the rate per period is
    (1 + g)^(1/m) − 1, with m the periods per year, so that m periods compound exactly to g;
    computed through the conversion of `rates.convert` (equivalent rate with compounding m,
    divided by m);
  - `annual_simple`: the rate g is an annual growth; the rate per period is g / m (proportional
    rate), so that m periods compound to (1 + g/m)^m − 1, which differs from g.
  For `linear`, the interpolation of R14 is made on the rates as given, then each rate is
  converted. The basis is required WHEN the timeline frequency has more than one period a year;
  WHEN the frequency is `annual`, the three bases give the same rates and the basis may be
  omitted; WHEN the timeline has no frequency, the rates are per period by definition, the basis
  may be omitted or `per_period`, and an annual basis SHALL return `FM-E001` naming
  `/growth_basis` and `/timeline` (no period length to convert to). Every case SHALL be stated in
  `explain.conventions`, with the effective annual growth implied by the per-period rate when
  the frequency is known.
- **R17** — THE ENGINE SHALL accept exactly one timeline form:
  - `periods`: a number of periods (1 to 10000) and an optional frequency (R3); no dates;
  - `schedule`: the `timeline` object returned by `dates.schedule` (frequency, start dates, end
    dates), passed unchanged. IF its start and end date lists differ in length, are empty, or
    the dates are not contiguous and strictly increasing (end of period k equal to start of
    period k + 1, each start before its end), THEN THE ENGINE SHALL return `FM-E001` naming the
    first offending element by its JSON pointer.
- **R18** — WHEN the timeline is `schedule`, THE ENGINE SHALL require a value timing,
  `end_of_period` or `beginning_of_period`, and SHALL return the dated values: one object per
  period with `date` (the end date or the start date of the period) and `amount` (the projected
  value), the same shape as an element of `cash_flows` in `tvm.xnpv` and `tvm.xirr`. WHEN the
  timeline is `periods`, a timing SHALL be refused with `FM-E001` (no dates to place values on).
- **R19** — THE ENGINE SHALL return, for every period: the period number, the start and end
  dates (dated timeline only), the growth rate as stated in the rule (before conversion), the
  growth rate per period applied (absent for period 1 under `period_1`), its implied effective
  annual growth when the frequency is known, and the value; and, as
  flat vectors for direct use, `values` (the values of periods 1..n, the shape of `cash_flows`
  in `tvm.present_value`) and, for a dated timeline, `dated_values` (R18).
- **R20** — IF a growth rate as given is −1 or lower, or a rate per period after conversion is
  −1 or lower, THEN THE ENGINE SHALL return `FM-E001` naming the rate by its JSON pointer (the
  rule of SPEC-0001 R11); IF a value overflows the decimal range, THEN it SHALL return
  `FM-E008`.
- **R21** — WHEN the base value is negative, THE ENGINE SHALL report in `explain.conventions`
  as a warning that a positive growth makes the values more negative, and SHALL NOT refuse it
  (as SPEC-0001 R11 for growth on a negative flow).
- **R22** — THE ENGINE SHALL include in `explain`: the recurrence stated once with its formula;
  the base value and the base period; the growth rule with its parameters; the conversion of
  the first growth rate with the numbers substituted (when a conversion applies); the first
  projected value with the numbers substituted; and the last value. The per-period numbers are
  carried by the rows; the number of steps is bounded and independent of n.

### Shared frequencies (compounding and payment frequency)
- **R23** — THE ENGINE SHALL define the frequencies of R3 (periods per year and months per
  period) in one place of the core, and the compounding list (`Compounding`, used by
  `rates.convert`, `tvm.xnpv`, `tvm.xirr` and `loans.*`) and the loan payment frequency
  (`PaymentFrequency`, used by `loans.*`) SHALL take their periodic values from it. The
  compounding list SHALL gain `bimonthly` (6 a year) and `four_monthly` (3 a year) next to
  `annual`, `semi_annual`, `quarterly`, `monthly` and `continuous`; the payment frequency SHALL
  gain the same two values.
- **R24** — WHEN a method that accepts a compounding or a payment frequency is called with
  `bimonthly` or `four_monthly`, THE ENGINE SHALL apply the same formulas as for the existing
  periodic values with m = 6 or m = 3 (growth (1 + r/m)^(m t), rate per compounding period
  r/m, `FM-E003` when r/m ≤ −1, loan periodic rate per SPEC-0009 R1), and SHALL label it in
  `explain.conventions`. Results for every existing value SHALL be unchanged, bit for bit.
- **R25** — The extension of R23 is an additive input change (new enumeration values, no
  existing input changes meaning, no result changes): per ADR 0009 §4 it SHALL NOT create new
  method versions; the current schema files of the affected methods SHALL be regenerated with
  the added values only (Open question 8).

### Common
- **R26** — WHEN a documented default is applied (in this spec, at most the end-of-month rule
  of R4, depending on Open question 1), THE ENGINE SHALL report it in `explain.conventions` and
  in `meta.defaults_applied` (the mechanism of #197), naming the field and the value used.
- **R27** — Every error SHALL name the offending field by its JSON pointer and state the fix in
  the message (ADR 0013). No new error code is introduced.

## Acceptance criteria

### dates.schedule
- **AC1** (R1, R2, R3, R4) — Golden cases, each cross-checked with python-dateutil
  (`start + relativedelta(months=k × m)` for `same_day`; `relativedelta(months=k × m, day=31)`
  for `month_end` when the start is a month end) and with QuantLib `Schedule` (null calendar,
  unadjusted, forward generation, `endOfMonth` matching the rule), source and library versions
  recorded in the golden file:
  - 2024-01-31, `monthly`, 4 periods, `same_day`: end dates 2024-02-29, 2024-03-31, 2024-04-30,
    2024-05-31;
  - 2023-01-31, `monthly`, 2 periods: 2023-02-28, 2023-03-31 (non-leap year, no drift);
  - 2025-06-30, `quarterly`, 4 periods: `same_day` 2025-09-30, 2025-12-30, 2026-03-30,
    2026-06-30; `month_end` 2025-09-30, 2025-12-31, 2026-03-31, 2026-06-30;
  - 2023-02-28, `monthly`, 3 periods: `same_day` 2023-03-28, 2023-04-28, 2023-05-28;
    `month_end` 2023-03-31, 2023-04-30, 2023-05-31;
  - 2024-02-29, `annual`, 4 periods, both rules: 2025-02-28, 2026-02-28, 2027-02-28, 2028-02-29
    (anchoring restores the 29th);
  - 2025-12-31, `quarterly`, 4 periods, both rules: 2026-03-31, 2026-06-30, 2026-09-30,
    2026-12-31;
  - 2025-01-31, `four_monthly`, 3 periods: 2025-05-31, 2025-09-30, 2026-01-31;
  - 2025-12-31, `bimonthly`, 6 periods: 2026-02-28, 2026-04-30, 2026-06-30, 2026-08-31,
    2026-10-31, 2026-12-31;
  - 2025-03-15, `semi_annual`, 3 periods: 2025-09-15, 2026-03-15, 2026-09-15.
- **AC2** (R1, R2, R4) — Property tests over random start dates (1900 to 2150), every frequency
  and both rules: n periods; start of period 1 equals the start date; end of period k equals
  start of period k + 1; dates strictly increasing; end of period n equals the date generated
  directly from the start date (no drift); when the start day is 28 or lower the two rules give
  identical schedules; the result equals the python-dateutil expression of AC1.
- **AC3** (R5) — With each of the five day counts, every period year fraction equals the output
  of `dates.year_fraction` for the same two dates exactly, and every year fraction from the start
  equals `dates.year_fraction` from the schedule start date to the period end exactly; under
  `act_365_fixed` the sum of the period year fractions equals the total days / 365 exactly; a
  golden case per day count is cross-checked with the QuantLib day counters (`Actual365Fixed`,
  `Actual360`, `ActualActual(ISDA)`, `Thirty360(BondBasis)`, `Thirty360(European)`). Without a
  day count no year-fraction field is present and `explain.conventions` says so.
- **AC4** (R6) — With `end_of_period` the payment dates equal the end dates, with
  `beginning_of_period` the start dates; without a timing no payment date is present;
  `mid_period` returns `FM-E001` listing the accepted values.
- **AC5** (R7, R17) — Contract test: the `timeline` object of every `dates.schedule` golden
  output validates against the `schedule` timeline of the `series.project` input schema and is
  accepted unchanged.
- **AC6** (R8, R9) — `number_of_periods` 0 and 10001, and the fields `end_date`, `calendar`,
  `business_day_convention` and `stub`, return `FM-E001` with the JSON pointer; start date
  `2023-02-29` returns `FM-E009` naming `/start_date`; start 2199-06-30 `annual` with 1 period
  returns `FM-E009` naming `/number_of_periods` and 2200-06-30.
- **AC7** (R10, R26) — Unit tests: `explain` contains every item of R10; the number of steps is
  the same for 4 and 400 periods; when a default is applied it appears in
  `explain.conventions` and in `meta.defaults_applied`.

### series.project
- **AC8** (R11, R12, R13) — Base 100, `constant` 0.05, `per_period` basis, 3 periods: under
  `period_0` values 105, 110.25, 115.7625; under `period_1` values 100, 105, 110.25 (exact).
- **AC9** (R14) — `linear` from 0.1308 to 0.05 over 5 steps (`period_0`, `per_period`): growth
  rates 0.11464, 0.09848, 0.08232, 0.06616, 0.05, which round to the published 11.46%, 9.85%,
  8.23%, 6.62%, 5.00% of Damodaran, *Investment Valuation*, 2nd edition, Illustration 15.3
  (Amgen); `linear` from 0.10 to 0.03 over 5 steps: 0.086, 0.072, 0.058, 0.044, 0.03 (SPEC-0001
  Decision 4). Values match the 50-digit reference.
- **AC10** (R15) — Under `period_1`, 4 periods, a `per_period` list of 4 rates returns
  `FM-E001` naming `/growth/per_period/growth_rates`, expected 3, actual 4, and the base period;
  a list of 3 matches the reference.
- **AC11** (R16) — Base 1000, `constant` 0.12, `monthly`, 12 periods, `period_0`:
  `annual_compound` gives a rate per period equal to the `rates.convert` result for 0.12 from
  `annual` to `monthly` divided by 12, and V_12 = 1120 within 1e-20 relative; `annual_simple`
  gives a rate per period of exactly 0.01 and V_12 = 1000 × 1.01^12 exactly as computed by the
  reference; the same for `bimonthly`, `quarterly`, `four_monthly` and `semi_annual`. A
  `monthly` timeline without a basis, and an annual basis on a `periods` timeline without a
  frequency, return `FM-E001` naming `/growth_basis`; an `annual` timeline without a basis is
  accepted and reported.
- **AC12** (R17, R18, R19) — A `schedule` timeline from AC1 (2025-12-31, `quarterly`, 4
  periods) with `end_of_period`: `dated_values` dates equal the end dates; with
  `beginning_of_period`, the start dates. Contract test: `dated_values` validates as the
  `cash_flows` of `tvm.xnpv` and `tvm.xirr`; an end-to-end golden case (an outlay of −1000 on
  2025-12-31 followed by the `dated_values`) gives the `tvm.xnpv` value of the same flows typed
  by hand. A `schedule` timeline with a gap, an overlap, unequal list lengths or empty lists
  returns `FM-E001` naming the first offending element; a timing with a `periods` timeline
  returns `FM-E001`.
- **AC13** (R11, R13, R14, R15) — Properties: `constant` g equals `per_period` with g repeated,
  bit for bit; `linear` with `from_growth` = `to_growth` equals `constant`; under `period_0` with
  `per_period` basis the values equal the flows of a `dcf.fcff` `constant_growth` and
  `linear_decline` stage with the same base flow and rates (SPEC-0001 R8, R9).
- **AC14** (R20, R21) — A `constant` growth of −1, a `per_period` element of −1.5 and an
  `annual_compound` growth of −1 on a monthly timeline return `FM-E001` naming the rate by its
  pointer; a negative base value is projected and the warning is reported; a value beyond the
  decimal range returns `FM-E008`.
- **AC15** (R22, R26, R27) — Unit tests: `explain` contains every item of R22 with a bounded
  number of steps; every error message contains a JSON pointer and the fix.

### Shared frequencies
- **AC16** (R23, R24) — `rates.convert` round-trips within 1e-20 for every pair of the seven
  compounding values (the existing test extended to `bimonthly` and `four_monthly`) and matches
  the 50-digit reference for 0.0735 from `annual` to `bimonthly` and to `four_monthly`;
  `tvm.xnpv` with `four_monthly` compounding matches the reference; `loans.instalment` with
  `payment_frequency` `bimonthly` gives the same instalment as the equivalent periodic rate in
  form (b) (SPEC-0009 AC12a); a rate of −3 with `four_monthly` returns `FM-E003` stating the
  rule r > −3.
- **AC17** (R24, R25) — Every existing golden case passes unchanged; no expected value is
  edited; `schemas/index.json` gains no version for `rates.convert`, `tvm.xnpv`, `tvm.xirr` or
  `loans.*`, and the schema check accepts the regenerated current files as additive.

## Interfaces
- **New method ids**: `dates.schedule` version 1 (theme `dates`, `crates/fairmark-core/src/dates.rs`)
  and `series.project` version 1 (new theme `series`, new module
  `crates/fairmark-core/src/series.rs`; the commit scope `series` is added to
  `scripts/conventions.py`). Both start as `preview` (ADR 0012) and are listed in
  `specs/method-evidence.yaml`.
- **`dates.schedule` input**: `start_date` (ISO 8601), `frequency`, `number_of_periods`
  (1 to 10000), `end_of_month` (`same_day` or `month_end`; Open question 1), `day_count`
  (optional), `timing` (optional, `end_of_period` or `beginning_of_period`).
- **`dates.schedule` output**: `timeline` {`frequency`, `start_dates`, `end_dates`},
  `periods_per_year`, `months_per_period`, `year_fractions` and `year_fractions_from_start`
  (with a day count), `payment_dates` (with a timing).
- **`series.project` input**: `base_value` (in the units of the series, for example currency
  units), `base_period` (`period_0` or `period_1`), `growth` (externally tagged, as the
  terminal technique of `dcf.fcff`: `{"constant": {"growth": g}}`,
  `{"linear": {"from_growth": a, "to_growth": b}}`,
  `{"per_period": {"growth_rates": [...]}}`; rates are decimal fractions), `growth_basis`
  (`per_period`, `annual_compound`, `annual_simple`; R16), `timeline`
  (`{"periods": {"number_of_periods": n, "frequency": f}}` with `frequency` optional, or
  `{"schedule": <timeline object of dates.schedule>}`), `timing` (dated timeline only).
- **`series.project` output**: `periods` (rows: `period`, `start_date` and `end_date` when dated,
  `stated_growth_rate`, `growth_rate`, `value`), `values` (periods 1..n), `dated_values`
  (dated timeline: `{date, amount}`, the element shape of `tvm.xnpv` `cash_flows`),
  `periods_per_year` (when a frequency is known); in each row, `implied_annual_growth`, the
  effective annual growth of the rate per period applied (when a frequency is known, R16).
- **Shared frequency change (additive)**: `Compounding` gains `bimonthly` and `four_monthly`;
  `PaymentFrequency` gains the same values; both derive their periods per year from the shared
  frequency definition of R23. Affected schemas (current versions regenerated, no new
  version): `rates.convert`, `tvm.xnpv`, `tvm.xirr`, `loans.instalment`,
  `loans.number_of_instalments`, `loans.amortisation_schedule`, and any other method whose input
  uses these types, found by the generator. ADR 0008 §4 lists the compounding options and
  needs an amendment note (Open question 8).
- **Errors**: no new code. `FM-E001` (counts, missing or conflicting choices, timing, timeline
  consistency, growth at or below −1), `FM-E003` (compounding rate r/m ≤ −1, unchanged rule),
  `FM-E008` (overflow), `FM-E009` (start date and generated dates out of range). The
  descriptions of `FM-E001` and `FM-E009` in `docs/en/reference/errors.md` gain the new cases.
- **Building blocks reused**: `Date`, `days_in_month` and `DayCount::year_fraction` (`dates`),
  `equivalent_rate` (`rates`), the timing names of `tvm` (`end_of_period`,
  `beginning_of_period`). Whether the shared frequency becomes the first piece of the timeline
  domain type of ADR 0009 §5 and issue #19 is a plan decision.
- **Python helpers**: `fm.dates.schedule` next to `dates.year_fraction`, new
  `python/fairmark/series.py` with `fm.series.project`.

## Open questions
- [ ] **1. Default of the end-of-month rule.** Options: (a) documented default `month_end`,
      reported in `explain.conventions` and `meta.defaults_applied`; (b) documented default
      `same_day` (spreadsheet EDATE behaviour); (c) required field. The rules differ only when
      the start is the last day of a month shorter than 31 days (30 June, 30 September, 28
      February). Recommendation: (a), because accounting periods end at month ends (30 June
      quarterly must reach 31 December) and it follows the market end-of-month convention;
      the maintainer confirms it is compatible with ADR 0008 §6 (defaults only where a
      published standard defines one), otherwise (c).
- [ ] **2. Day count absent.** Options: (a) no year fractions and a stated convention; (b)
      documented default `act_365_fixed`, as `tvm.xnpv`. Recommendation: (a): the dates are the
      result, `tvm.xnpv` computes its own times from them, and a default day count would
      produce numbers on an assumption the user did not state (ADR 0008 §6).
- [ ] **3. Names of the two new frequencies.** Options: (a) `bimonthly` and `four_monthly` with
      unambiguous schema descriptions; (b) `every_two_months` and `every_four_months`
      (descriptive, after QuantLib `EveryFourthMonth`). Recommendation: (a), the names requested,
      with "every two months, 6 periods a year" and "every four months, 3 periods a year" in the
      schema, subject to finding a recognised source for "four-monthly"; otherwise (b).
- [ ] **4. Base period.** Options: (a) required, no default; (b) documented default `period_0`
      (the DCF convention). Recommendation: (a): the two readings differ by one full period of
      growth on every value and neither is a market standard.
- [ ] **5. Linear rule.** Options: (a) g_j = g_from + (g_to − g_from) × j / s, `from_growth`
      is the growth before the first step (SPEC-0001 Decision 4); (b) g_1 = `from_growth`,
      g_s = `to_growth`, step (j − 1) / (s − 1). Recommendation: (a), so that a series equals
      a DCF `linear_decline` stage and s = 1 is defined; the methodology page shows both
      readings with the "from 10% to 3% over 5" example.
- [ ] **6. Timeline forms.** Options: (a) `periods` and `schedule` (the `timeline` object
      returned by `dates.schedule`); (b) also the `dates.schedule` input embedded, so that one
      call builds dates and values. Recommendation: (a) in version 1, (b) later as an additive
      option if agents ask for it.
- [ ] **7. Name of the dated output.** Options: (a) `dated_values`, element shape `{date,
      amount}` of `tvm.xnpv` `cash_flows`; (b) `cash_flows`. Recommendation: (a): a projected
      series is often revenue or cost, not a cash flow; the agent copies the list into
      `cash_flows`.
- [ ] **8. Versioning of the compounding extension.** Options: (a) additive change of the
      current versions per ADR 0009 §4, with an amendment note to ADR 0008 §4 approved by the
      maintainer; (b) new versions of every affected method. Recommendation: (a). If the CI
      schema check classifies an enumeration extension as non-additive, the implementation
      stops and the maintainer decides.
- [ ] **9. Error code for count and timeline mismatches.** Options: (a) `FM-E001` with expected
      and actual counts; (b) widen `FM-E025`, today specific to DCF stages. Recommendation: (a).
- [ ] **10. Generated date after 2199-12-31.** Options: (a) `FM-E009` naming
      `/number_of_periods`; (b) `FM-E001`. Recommendation: (a): the cause is the supported date
      range.
- [ ] **11. Dependency on #197.** `meta.defaults_applied` is introduced by #197. Recommendation:
      implement this spec after #197 is merged; if it is not, report defaults in
      `explain.conventions` only and add them to `meta.defaults_applied` when #197 lands.
