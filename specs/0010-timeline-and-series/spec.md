## Problem
Analysts build a model from a start date, a frequency and a number of periods; the model then
needs the period dates, the length of each period and a row of values grown from a base. An
AI agent asked to do this today must compute the dates itself (month ends, February, leap
years) and the projected values itself, before passing them to `tvm.xnpv` or `tvm.xirr`. These
are the computations where errors are silent: 31 January plus one month, a schedule that
drifts from the 31st to the 28th, a period end read as inclusive in one place and exclusive in
another, an annual growth applied unchanged to monthly periods, a first value that is or is
not already grown. `dates.schedule` builds the periods as financial markets define them, with a
table that names each period by calendar and fiscal year, half, quarter and month so that the
analyst can reconcile it with the accounts; `series.project` builds the values on the same
periods, with outputs that feed `tvm.xnpv`, `tvm.xirr` and, later, the DCF with dated periods
(#167).

## Scope
**In scope:**
- `dates.schedule`: from a start date, a frequency and a number of periods, a period table:
  index, start date, end date (accrual period [start, end)), year fraction under a stated day
  count, calendar labels (year, half, quarter, month), fiscal labels (fiscal year, fiscal
  quarter) and a reference year and quarter for every period.
- `series.project`: from a base value, a growth rule (constant, linear between two rates, one
  rate per period) and a timeline (a number of periods with an optional frequency, or a
  schedule defined as for `dates.schedule`), the projected value of every period; conversion of
  an annual growth rate to a sub-annual period with an explicit convention.
- Frequencies of 12, 6, 4, 3, 2 and 1 periods a year, defined once in the core and shared by
  the compounding list (`rates.convert`, `tvm.xnpv`, `tvm.xirr`, `loans.*`) and the loan payment
  frequency (`loans.*`).

**Out of scope (future extensions, designed to be additive):**
- Business-day calendars and business-day conventions: every date of version 1 is unadjusted.
- A termination date instead of a number of periods; stub periods; backward generation.
- Frequencies that are not whole months, and frequencies longer than one year.
- Payment dates distinct from period dates, mid-period dates, payment lags.
- Rounding of projected values; growth rules other than the three above.
- Any change to `dcf.*`: the DCF with dated periods is specified by #167.

## User stories
- As an analyst, I want the quarterly periods of 2026 from 1 January 2026, each labelled 2026 Q1
  to Q4 with its half, so that the table reconciles with my quarterly accounts.
- As an analyst, I want a monthly schedule that starts on 31 January to keep month ends (29
  February, then 31 March) and never drift to the 29th, so that the dates match my ledger.
- As an analyst with a fiscal year starting in April, I want each period labelled with its
  fiscal year and fiscal quarter, so that the plan matches the company's reporting calendar.
- As an analyst, I want revenues of 1,000 grown at 12% a year, projected monthly, with the
  annual-to-monthly conversion stated, so that I know whether twelve months compound to 12%.
- As an AI agent, I want one unambiguous meaning of a period's end date, stated in the method
  description and the schema, so that I never count the end date twice.
- As an AI agent, I want `series.project` to return dated values in the shape of the
  `cash_flows` of `tvm.xnpv`, so that I never retype a date or a number.

## Requirements (EARS)

### dates.schedule: periods
- **R1** — WHEN given a start date, a frequency and a number of periods n, THE ENGINE SHALL
  return n contiguous accrual periods. Period k runs from and including its start date to but
  excluding its end date, [start_k, end_k), as the calculation periods of the 2006 ISDA
  Definitions; start_1 is the start date; end_k = d_k (R2); start_{k+1} = end_k. The end date of
  a period is therefore the first day of the next period and is not part of the period; there
  is no inclusive "last day" field. THE ENGINE SHALL state this definition in the method
  description, in the schema description of the start and end date fields and in
  `explain.conventions`.
- **R2** — THE ENGINE SHALL generate every date from the start date, not from the previous
  date: d_k = start date + k × (months per period) months, so that a day shortened in one
  period (31 January to 29 February) is restored in the next (31 March) and the schedule never
  drifts. The day of month SHALL follow the end-of-month rule, which is fixed: WHEN the start
  date is the last day of its month, every date is the last day of its month (30 June quarterly:
  30 September, 31 December, 31 March, 30 June); otherwise every date keeps the day of the start
  date, or the last day of the month when that month is shorter (30 January monthly: 28
  February, 30 March). This is the end-of-month convention of ISDA and ICMA practice; THE ENGINE
  SHALL report in `explain.conventions` whether the start date is a month end and whether the
  rule changed any date.
- **R3** — THE ENGINE SHALL accept the frequencies `monthly` (12 periods a year, 1 month per
  period), `bimonthly` (6, 2 months), `quarterly` (4, 3 months), `four_monthly` (3, 4 months),
  `semi_annual` (2, 6 months) and `annual` (1, 12 months), and SHALL return the periods per year
  and the months per period.
- **R4** — WHEN a day count (the five day counts of SPEC-0002 R2) is given, THE ENGINE SHALL
  return the year fraction of every period from its start date to its end date, computed by the
  day-count function of `dates.year_fraction` on [start, end) (no second implementation). WHEN
  no day count is given, THE ENGINE SHALL return no year fraction and SHALL state in
  `explain.conventions` that none was computed (Open question 1).
- **R5** — THE ENGINE SHALL produce unadjusted dates and SHALL report it in
  `explain.conventions`; fields for an end date, a calendar, a business-day convention or a
  stub are not part of version 1 and SHALL be refused as unknown fields (`FM-E001`).

### dates.schedule: period table and labels
- **R6** — THE ENGINE SHALL return a period table with one row per period: `period` (1..n),
  `start_date`, `end_date`, `year_fraction` (with a day count), the calendar labels
  `calendar_year`, `calendar_half` (1 or 2), `calendar_quarter` (1 to 4), `calendar_month` (1 to
  12), the fiscal labels `fiscal_year`, `fiscal_quarter` (1 to 4), the reference labels
  `reference_year`, `reference_quarter`, and `label_note` when a label is null.
- **R7** — Calendar labels SHALL be given only for a period aligned to the calendar grid of its
  frequency: it starts on the first day of a month whose position from January is a multiple of
  the months per period (any month for `monthly`; January, March, May, July, September,
  November for `bimonthly`; January, April, July, October for `quarterly`; January, May,
  September for `four_monthly`; January, July for `semi_annual`; January for `annual`). For an
  aligned period, each label is the unit (year, half, quarter, month) that contains the whole
  period, and is null when the period is longer than that unit or spans two of them (for
  example the month of a quarterly period, or the quarter of the bimonthly period March to
  April). For a period that is not aligned, every calendar label SHALL be null.
- **R8** — WHEN `fiscal_year_start_month` (1 to 12) is given, THE ENGINE SHALL compute the
  fiscal labels by R7 on the grid that starts in that month: the fiscal year runs from the first
  day of that month for twelve months and is named by the calendar year in which it ends (Open
  question 6); fiscal quarters are counted from its start. WHEN it is not given, the fiscal year
  equals the calendar year, the fiscal labels equal the calendar year and quarter, and
  `explain.conventions` SHALL say so. IF it is outside 1 to 12, THEN THE ENGINE SHALL return
  `FM-E001` naming `/fiscal_year_start_month`.
- **R9** — THE ENGINE SHALL give every period, aligned or not, a reference year and quarter: the
  calendar year and quarter of the last day of the period, which is the day before its end date
  (Open question 5). For an aligned period they equal its calendar labels whenever these are
  given. WHEN any label of a row is null, `label_note` SHALL state which labels are null and why
  (not aligned, with the start day and month; longer than the unit; spans two units).

### dates.schedule: errors and explain
- **R10** — IF the number of periods is 0 or above 10000, THEN THE ENGINE SHALL return
  `FM-E001` naming `/number_of_periods`; IF the start date is malformed, impossible or out of
  range, THEN it SHALL return `FM-E009` naming `/start_date`; IF a generated date falls after
  2199-12-31, THEN it SHALL return `FM-E009` naming `/number_of_periods`, the last valid period
  and the first date out of range.
- **R11** — THE ENGINE SHALL include in `explain`: the period definition [start, end); the
  generation rule stated once with its formula; the frequency with its periods per year and
  months per period; the end-of-month rule and its effect; the day count, or the statement that
  none was given; the fiscal year convention; the labelling rule; and, with a day count, the
  year fraction of the first period with the numbers substituted (the formula of
  `dates.year_fraction`) and the sum of the year fractions. The rows carry the per-period
  numbers, as in `loans.amortisation_schedule` (SPEC-0009 Decision 8); the number of steps is
  bounded and independent of n.

### series.project
- **R12** — WHEN given a base value V_0, a growth rule, a base period and a timeline of n
  periods, THE ENGINE SHALL return the projected value of every period 1..n by the recurrence
  V_k = V_{k−1} × (1 + g_k), computed period by period (never by a closed-form power), where
  g_k is the growth rate per period into period k after any conversion (R16).
- **R13** — THE ENGINE SHALL require the base period, with no default:
  - `period_0`: the base value is the value before the first period (for example the last
    actual year); V_1 = V_0 × (1 + g_1); n growth steps;
  - `period_1`: the base value is the value of period 1; V_1 = V_0; growth steps into periods
    2..n only (n − 1 steps).
  The returned values are always those of periods 1..n.
- **R14** — WHEN the growth rule is `constant` with rate g, THE ENGINE SHALL apply g to every
  growth step. WHEN it is `linear` from `from_growth` to `to_growth` over s growth steps, THE
  ENGINE SHALL apply g_j = from_growth + (to_growth − from_growth) × j / s for j = 1..s, so that
  `to_growth` is reached in the last step and `from_growth` is the growth before the first step
  (SPEC-0001 Decision 4; Open question 4); the rule applies to rising and falling growth.
- **R15** — WHEN the growth rule is `per_period`, THE ENGINE SHALL apply the listed rates in
  order, one per growth step; IF the list does not have exactly s elements, THEN it SHALL return
  `FM-E001` naming `/growth/per_period/growth_rates` with the expected and the actual count and
  the base period that sets the count.
- **R16** — THE ENGINE SHALL interpret the rates of the rule by the growth basis:
  - `per_period`: the growth per period of the timeline, used as given;
  - `annual_compound`: an annual growth g, converted to (1 + g)^(1/m) − 1 per period, with m
    the periods per year, so that m periods compound exactly to g; computed through the
    conversion of `rates.convert` (equivalent rate with compounding m, divided by m);
  - `annual_simple`: an annual growth g, converted to g / m per period (proportional rate), so
    that m periods compound to (1 + g/m)^m − 1, which differs from g.
  For `linear`, interpolation is made on the rates as given, then each rate is converted. The
  basis is required WHEN the timeline frequency has more than one period a year; it may be
  omitted WHEN the frequency is `annual` (the three bases coincide) or WHEN the timeline has no
  frequency (the rates are per period by definition); an annual basis on a timeline without a
  frequency SHALL return `FM-E001` naming `/growth_basis`. The case that applied SHALL be stated
  in `explain.conventions`, with the effective annual growth implied by each rate per period
  when the frequency is known.
- **R17** — THE ENGINE SHALL accept exactly one timeline form:
  - `periods`: a number of periods (1 to 10000) and an optional frequency (R3); no dates;
  - `schedule`: a schedule definition, the same object as the input of `dates.schedule`; the
    periods are built by the `dates.schedule` function (typed call, ADR 0009), so the dates
    are identical by construction and nothing is copied or re-validated.
- **R18** — WHEN the timeline is `schedule`, THE ENGINE SHALL require a value timing,
  `end_of_period` (the value is dated at the period end date, which is the first day of the next
  period, R1) or `beginning_of_period` (dated at the period start date), and SHALL return
  `dated_values`: one `{date, amount}` per period, the element shape of `cash_flows` in
  `tvm.xnpv` and `tvm.xirr`. WHEN the timeline is `periods`, a timing SHALL be refused with
  `FM-E001`. The methodology page SHALL show that end-of-period values of a schedule starting on
  1 January are dated 1 April, 1 July, and so on, and that a schedule starting on 31 December
  dates them on 31 March, 30 June, 30 September and 31 December.
- **R19** — THE ENGINE SHALL return one row per period with `period`, the rate as stated in the
  rule (before conversion), the rate per period applied (absent for period 1 under `period_1`),
  its implied effective annual growth when the frequency is known, and `value`; with a
  `schedule` timeline every row also carries the fields of the `dates.schedule` row (R6). It
  SHALL also return `values`, the values of periods 1..n as a flat list (the shape of
  `cash_flows` in `tvm.present_value`).
- **R20** — IF a growth rate as given, or a rate per period after conversion, is −1 or lower,
  THEN THE ENGINE SHALL return `FM-E001` naming the rate by its JSON pointer (the rule of
  SPEC-0001 R11); IF a value overflows the decimal range, THEN it SHALL return `FM-E008`.
- **R21** — WHEN the base value is negative, THE ENGINE SHALL report in `explain.conventions` as
  a warning that a positive growth makes the values more negative, and SHALL NOT refuse it
  (SPEC-0001 R11).
- **R22** — THE ENGINE SHALL include in `explain`: the recurrence stated once with its formula;
  the base value and base period; the growth rule with its parameters; the conversion of the
  first rate with the numbers substituted (when a conversion applies); the first projected value
  with the numbers substituted; the last value; and, with a `schedule` timeline, the schedule
  conventions of R11. The number of steps is bounded and independent of n.

### Shared frequencies (compounding and payment frequency)
- **R23** — THE ENGINE SHALL define the frequencies of R3 (periods per year and months per
  period) in one place of the core; the compounding list (`Compounding`, used by
  `rates.convert`, `tvm.xnpv`, `tvm.xirr` and `loans.*`) and the loan payment frequency
  (`PaymentFrequency`, used by `loans.*`) SHALL take their periodic values from it. Both SHALL
  gain `bimonthly` (6 a year) and `four_monthly` (3 a year).
- **R24** — WHEN a method that accepts a compounding or a payment frequency is called with
  `bimonthly` or `four_monthly`, THE ENGINE SHALL apply the formulas of the existing periodic
  values with m = 6 or m = 3 (growth (1 + r/m)^(m t), `FM-E003` when r/m ≤ −1, loan periodic
  rate per SPEC-0009 R1) and label it in `explain.conventions`. Results for every existing value
  SHALL be unchanged, bit for bit.
- **R25** — The extension of R23 is an additive input change (new enumeration values; no input
  changes meaning; no result changes): per ADR 0009 §4 it SHALL NOT create new method versions;
  the current schema files of the affected methods SHALL be regenerated with the added values
  only (Open question 7).

### Common
- **R26** — No documented default is introduced by this spec: every choice is either required,
  fixed and reported (end-of-month rule, unadjusted dates, period definition), or optional with
  a stated effect of its absence (day count, fiscal year start, growth basis under R16). WHEN a
  later version adds a default, it SHALL be reported in `explain.conventions` and in
  `meta.defaults_applied` (#197).
- **R27** — Every error SHALL name the offending field by its JSON pointer and state the fix
  (ADR 0013). No new error code is introduced.

## Acceptance criteria

### dates.schedule
- **AC1** (R1, R2, R3) — Golden cases, each cross-checked with QuantLib `Schedule` (null
  calendar, `Unadjusted`, forward generation, `endOfMonth` true) and with python-dateutil
  (`start + relativedelta(months=k × m, day=31)` when the start is a month end, `start +
  relativedelta(months=k × m)` otherwise), library versions recorded in the golden file; end
  dates:
  - 2024-01-31 `monthly`, 4: 2024-02-29, 2024-03-31, 2024-04-30, 2024-05-31;
  - 2025-01-30 `monthly`, 3: 2025-02-28, 2025-03-30, 2025-04-30 (not a month end: anchored, no
    end-of-month rule);
  - 2025-06-30 `quarterly`, 4: 2025-09-30, 2025-12-31, 2026-03-31, 2026-06-30;
  - 2023-02-28 `monthly`, 3: 2023-03-31, 2023-04-30, 2023-05-31;
  - 2024-02-29 `annual`, 4: 2025-02-28, 2026-02-28, 2027-02-28, 2028-02-29;
  - 2025-12-31 `quarterly`, 4: 2026-03-31, 2026-06-30, 2026-09-30, 2026-12-31;
  - 2025-01-31 `four_monthly`, 3: 2025-05-31, 2025-09-30, 2026-01-31;
  - 2025-12-31 `bimonthly`, 6: 2026-02-28, 2026-04-30, 2026-06-30, 2026-08-31, 2026-10-31,
    2026-12-31;
  - 2025-03-15 `semi_annual`, 3: 2025-09-15, 2026-03-15, 2026-09-15.
- **AC2** (R1, R2) — Property tests over random start dates (1900 to 2150) and every frequency:
  n rows; start of period 1 equals the start date; end of period k equals start of period k + 1;
  dates strictly increasing; end of period n equals the date generated directly from the start
  date (no drift); the result equals the python-dateutil expression of AC1.
- **AC3** (R4) — With each of the five day counts, every row's year fraction equals
  `dates.year_fraction(start_date, end_date)` exactly; under `act_365_fixed` the sum equals the
  total days / 365 exactly; one golden case per day count is cross-checked with the QuantLib day
  counters (`Actual365Fixed`, `Actual360`, `ActualActual(ISDA)`, `Thirty360(BondBasis)`,
  `Thirty360(European)`). Without a day count no row has `year_fraction` and
  `explain.conventions` says so.
- **AC4** (R6, R7, R9) — Calendar labels, golden cases:
  - 2026-01-01 `quarterly`, 4: years 2026; halves 1, 1, 2, 2; quarters 1, 2, 3, 4; months null
    with a note "longer than a month"; reference 2026 Q1 to Q4; end dates 2026-04-01, 2026-07-01,
    2026-10-01, 2027-01-01;
  - 2026-01-01 `monthly`, 12: months 1 to 12, quarters 1, 1, 1, 2, …, halves 1 and 2;
  - 2026-03-01 `bimonthly`, 3: aligned; quarter null for March to April and May to June (spans
    two quarters), quarter 3 for July to August; halves 1, 1, 2;
  - 2025-12-31 `quarterly`, 4: not aligned, every calendar label null with a note naming the
    start day 31; reference 2026 Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4;
  - 2026-02-01 `bimonthly`, 2: not aligned (February does not start a two-month block from
    January), calendar labels null; periods [2026-02-01, 2026-04-01) and [2026-04-01,
    2026-06-01); reference 2026 Q1 (last day 2026-03-31) and 2026 Q2 (last day 2026-05-31).
- **AC5** (R8) — `fiscal_year_start_month` 4: 2025-04-01 `quarterly`, 4: fiscal year 2026 in
  every row, fiscal quarters 1 to 4, calendar quarters 2, 3, 4 of 2025 and 1 of 2026;
  2025-04-01 `annual`, 2: calendar labels null (not aligned to January), fiscal years 2026 and
  2027, fiscal quarter null; without the field, fiscal labels equal calendar year and quarter
  and the convention is reported; 0 and 13 return `FM-E001`.
- **AC6** (R5, R10) — `number_of_periods` 0 and 10001, and the fields `end_date`, `calendar`,
  `business_day_convention` and `stub`, return `FM-E001` with the JSON pointer; start date
  `2023-02-29` returns `FM-E009` naming `/start_date`; 2199-06-30 `annual` with 1 period returns
  `FM-E009` naming `/number_of_periods` and 2200-06-30.
- **AC7** (R1, R11) — Unit tests: the registry description of `dates.schedule` and the schema
  descriptions of `start_date` and `end_date` contain "from and including" and "to but
  excluding"; `explain` contains every item of R11; the number of steps is the same for 4 and
  400 periods.

### series.project
- **AC8** (R12, R13, R14) — Base 100, `constant` 0.05, 3 periods: `period_0` gives 105, 110.25,
  115.7625; `period_1` gives 100, 105, 110.25 (exact).
- **AC9** (R14) — `linear` from 0.1308 to 0.05 over 5 steps (`period_0`): rates 0.11464,
  0.09848, 0.08232, 0.06616, 0.05, which round to the published 11.46%, 9.85%, 8.23%, 6.62%,
  5.00% of Damodaran, *Investment Valuation*, 2nd edition, Illustration 15.3 (Amgen); from 0.10
  to 0.03 over 5 steps: 0.086, 0.072, 0.058, 0.044, 0.03 (SPEC-0001 Decision 4). Values match the
  50-digit reference.
- **AC10** (R15) — Under `period_1`, 4 periods, a list of 4 rates returns `FM-E001` naming
  `/growth/per_period/growth_rates`, expected 3, actual 4; a list of 3 matches the reference.
- **AC11** (R16) — Base 1000, `constant` 0.12, `monthly`, 12 periods, `period_0`:
  `annual_compound` gives a rate per period equal to `rates.convert` of 0.12 from `annual` to
  `monthly` divided by 12, and V_12 = 1120 within 1e-20 relative; `annual_simple` gives 0.01 per
  period and V_12 equal to the reference value of 1000 × 1.01^12 exactly; the same for every
  sub-annual frequency. A `monthly` timeline without a basis, and an annual basis on a `periods`
  timeline without a frequency, return `FM-E001` naming `/growth_basis`; an `annual` timeline
  without a basis is accepted and reported.
- **AC12** (R17, R18, R19) — A `schedule` timeline equal to the input of the AC4 case
  2026-01-01 `quarterly`, 4 gives rows whose schedule fields equal the `dates.schedule` rows;
  with `end_of_period` the `dated_values` dates are 2026-04-01, 2026-07-01, 2026-10-01,
  2027-01-01; with `beginning_of_period` the start dates. Contract test: `dated_values` validates
  as the `cash_flows` of `tvm.xnpv` and `tvm.xirr`; an end-to-end golden case (an outlay of −1000
  on 2026-01-01 followed by the `dated_values`) gives the `tvm.xnpv` value of the same flows
  typed by hand. A timing with a `periods` timeline returns `FM-E001`.
- **AC13** (R12 to R15) — Properties: `constant` g equals `per_period` with g repeated, bit for
  bit; `linear` with equal ends equals `constant`; under `period_0` with `per_period` basis the
  values equal the flows of a `dcf.fcff` `constant_growth` or `linear_decline` stage with the
  same base flow and rates (SPEC-0001 R8, R9).
- **AC14** (R20, R21) — A `constant` growth of −1, a `per_period` element of −1.5 and an
  `annual_compound` growth of −1 on a monthly timeline return `FM-E001` naming the rate; a
  negative base value is projected and the warning reported; a value beyond the decimal range
  returns `FM-E008`.
- **AC15** (R22, R27) — Unit tests: `explain` contains every item of R22 with a bounded number
  of steps; every error message contains a JSON pointer and the fix.

### Shared frequencies
- **AC16** (R23, R24) — `rates.convert` round-trips within 1e-20 for every pair of the seven
  compounding values and matches the 50-digit reference for 0.0735 from `annual` to `bimonthly`
  and to `four_monthly`; `tvm.xnpv` with `four_monthly` compounding matches the reference;
  `loans.instalment` with `payment_frequency` `bimonthly` equals the equivalent periodic rate in
  form (b) (SPEC-0009 AC12a); a rate of −3 with `four_monthly` returns `FM-E003` stating r > −3.
- **AC17** (R24, R25) — Every existing golden case passes unchanged; no expected value is
  edited; `schemas/index.json` gains no version for the affected methods and the schema check
  accepts the regenerated current files as additive.

## Decisions (maintainer, 2026-11-08)
- [x] **A. Market practice.** Financial-market practice (ISDA) is followed, not spreadsheet
      conventions.
- [x] **B. Accrual periods.** Periods are [start, end); the end date is the start of the next
      period; no inclusive last-day field; year fractions on [start, end) (R1, R4).
- [x] **C. Period table.** `dates.schedule` returns one row per period with index, dates, year
      fraction, calendar and fiscal labels; labels only for aligned periods, null with a reason
      otherwise, with the year and quarter that name the period (R6 to R9).
- [x] **D. End-of-month rule.** Anchored to the start date, with the ISDA and ICMA end-of-month
      convention. Applied as one fixed, reported rule with no input field, which also resolves
      the former question on its default; a `same_day` option can be added later as an
      additive value if a user needs it (R2).
- [x] **E. Simplification of this revision.** Removed as redundant: the payment timing and
      payment dates of `dates.schedule` (equal to the start or end dates of the table); the
      separate date and year-fraction vectors (carried by the rows); the year fraction from
      the start (given by `dates.year_fraction`); the `timeline` output object and its
      contiguity checks in `series.project` (replaced by the schedule definition, R17).

## Open questions
- [ ] **1. Day count absent.** Options: (a) no year fraction, stated in the conventions; (b)
      documented default `act_365_fixed`, as `tvm.xnpv`. Recommendation: (a): the dates are the
      result, and a default day count would produce numbers on an assumption the user did not
      state (ADR 0008 §6).
- [ ] **2. Names of the two new frequencies.** Options: (a) `bimonthly` and `four_monthly` with
      unambiguous schema descriptions; (b) `every_two_months` and `every_four_months` (after
      QuantLib `EveryFourthMonth`). Recommendation: (a), subject to a recognised source for
      "four-monthly"; otherwise (b).
- [ ] **3. Base period.** Options: (a) required; (b) documented default `period_0`.
      Recommendation: (a): the readings differ by a full period of growth on every value.
- [ ] **4. Linear rule.** Options: (a) step j / s, `from_growth` before the first step
      (SPEC-0001 Decision 4); (b) g_1 = `from_growth`, g_s = `to_growth`, step (j − 1) / (s − 1).
      Recommendation: (a), so that a series equals a DCF `linear_decline` stage and s = 1 is
      defined.
- [ ] **5. Date that names a period (reference year and quarter).** The maintainer recommended
      the end date, as reporting periods are named by their end. Options: (a) the end date;
      (b) the last day of the period, end date − 1 day. Recommendation: (b), which keeps the
      intent: under [start, end) the end date belongs to the next period, so (a) would name
      [1 February, 1 April) as Q2 although it covers February and March, and [1 April 2025,
      1 April 2026) as 2026 Q2; for month-end schedules (31 December, 31 March, …), the usual
      non-aligned case, (a) and (b) give the same year and quarter.
- [ ] **6. Fiscal year name.** Options: (a) the calendar year in which the fiscal year ends
      (April 2025 to March 2026 is 2026); (b) the year in which it starts. Recommendation: (a),
      as financial statements are identified by their closing date ("year ended 31 March 2026");
      the convention is reported in every result.
- [ ] **7. Versioning of the compounding extension.** Options: (a) additive change of the
      current versions (ADR 0009 §4) with an amendment note to ADR 0008 §4; (b) new versions of
      every affected method. Recommendation: (a); if the CI schema check classifies an
      enumeration extension as non-additive, the implementation stops and the maintainer
      decides.
- [ ] **8. Timeline of `series.project`.** The draft passed the dates back from the output of
      `dates.schedule`; this revision takes the schedule definition instead (R17), so the dates
      are identical by construction and no consistency check or copying is needed.
      Recommendation: confirm.
- [ ] **9. Error codes.** Count mismatches use `FM-E001` (not a widened `FM-E025`, which is
      specific to DCF stages); a generated date after 2199-12-31 uses `FM-E009`.
      Recommendation: confirm both.
- [ ] **10. Order with #197.** No default is introduced (R26), so this spec does not depend on
      #197. Recommendation: implement independently.
